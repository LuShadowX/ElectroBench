Course and scope

The project is built on the ECE131 Basic Electrical and Electronics Engineering syllabus for session 2026-27: all six units and all six course outcomes are covered, and chapters 04 to 08 follow the syllabus unit by unit. ECE131 is a theory course (L:3 T:1 P:0), so the practical side is covered by the seven experiments of the ECE279 lab, each with a virtual bench. The same course was earlier offered as ECE249, which had more digital electronics (number systems, combinational and sequential circuits, counters); those topics are built too and shown in chapter 10, so the project matches either version of the course.

01 · Project overview

Learning electronics by changing it

In a textbook a circuit is a fixed picture with one set of values. ElectroBench turns every circuit into something you can change: move a slider and the mesh currents, the diode curve, the filter's cut-off or the flip-flop's output react immediately. The lab experiments get a virtual bench with a power supply, multimeter, CRO and TTL chips, so the procedure can be practised before (or after) the real lab.

What makes it different from a set of notes

  • The models are real calculations. The DC page solves the network with nodal analysis, the diode bench solves the Shockley equation for the operating point, and the rectifier page simulates the capacitor charging and discharging in time.
  • Digital circuits are simulated at gate level with gate delays. Every adder, multiplexer and flip-flop is a netlist of gates. Because each gate has a delay, a JK flip-flop with the clock held high really oscillates: the race-around condition is something you see, not just read.
  • Experiments produce a lab record. Readings taken on the bench are saved and fill in the observation tables, the result is written from those readings, and the Lab record page prints all seven experiments as one file.

Technology

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript (ES modules). There is no framework, no build step and no server. Drawings are SVG generated in code, the CRO screen is a canvas, and progress and readings are stored in the browser's local storage. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages.

09 · Lab (ECE279)

Seven experiments on a virtual bench

ECE131 has no practical hours of its own, so the hands-on part follows the ECE279 lab that goes with the course: the KVL and diode experiments support Units I and IV, and the Arduino experiment supports Unit VI. Every experiment follows the format of a lab file. Aim and theory gives the aim, apparatus, theory, procedure and precautions. Virtual bench is where the readings are taken. Observations and result fills the tables from those readings and writes the result. Viva questions lists questions asked in the viva, with answers.

EXP 1Introduction to components and instruments

A component gallery (passive and active), a colour-code reader where you choose the bands and "measure" the resistor (the reading has a realistic tolerance error), a breadboard where clicking a hole highlights everything connected to it, a regulated supply with a DMM, and a function generator connected to a CRO with volts/div, time/div and Autoset. Readings go into three tables.

EXP 2KVL voltage hunt

A T-network on a 10 V supply: R1 in series with R2 ∥ R3, values chosen from E12. The actual resistors differ from their marked values by up to ±4%, and the meter has a little noise, so the measured loop sums come out close to zero but not exactly, like in the real lab. Place the red and black probes on nodes to measure any voltage.

EXP 3Diode V-I characteristics (1N4007)

The supply drives the diode through 100 Ω. The diode voltage and current are the solution of the Shockley equation with the loop equation, found by bisection. Readings can be taken one at a time or as a full set; the graph, knee voltage, static and dynamic resistance are worked out from the recorded points.

EXP 4Universal gates (7400, 7402)

NOT, AND and OR built from NAND only and from NOR only. For each circuit the IC pinout is drawn with the pins you wire highlighted and labelled, next to a connection table and the live gate circuit. "Record this row" saves the LED state for the current switches; the observation table marks each row ✓ or ✗ against the expected value.

09 · Lab (ECE279, continued)

EXP 5Half and full adder (7486, 7408, 7432)

The pin wiring of all three chips for the full adder: gate 1 of the 7486 makes A⊕B, gate 2 adds Cin; the two AND gates of the 7408 make AB and Cin(A⊕B); the 7432 ORs them into Cout.

EXP 6JK flip-flop using NAND gates (7400, 7410)

The steering gates are 3-input NANDs from the 7410 (J, CLK, Q' and K, CLK, Q), and the latch is two NANDs of the 7400. A short clock pulse (two gate delays) lets the flip-flop change exactly once, so the truth table can be recorded: hold, reset, set, toggle. Holding the clock high with J = K = 1 shows the race-around condition and records how many times Q changed.

EXP 7Arduino projects

Three sense → decide → act projects with the full sketch, the connection table, a block diagram, the serial monitor and a picture that follows the same logic as the code: an automatic street light (LDR on A0, threshold 300, relay on pin 7, status LED on 13), a temperature-controlled vent (DHT11 on pin 2, servo on pin 5 opening to 90° above 28 °C) and a parking distance alarm (HC-SR04 trig 9 / echo 10, buzzer on pin 8 below 20 cm).

Lab record

The Lab record page puts the aim, apparatus, observation tables and result of all seven experiments into one document. It has print styles, so "Print / save as PDF" gives a clean lab file without the sidebar.

12 · Code walkthrough

How the important parts work

Gate-level simulation with delays

logic.js · lines 47–57step() {
    const next = {};
    let changed = false;
    for (const g of this.gates) {
        const v = OPS[g.type](g.in.map(sig => this.get(sig))) ? 1 : 0;
        next[g.id] = v;
        if (v !== this.values[g.id]) changed = true;
    }
    Object.assign(this.values, next);
    return changed;
}

Every gate computes its new output from the values of the previous instant, then all outputs change together. That is one gate delay.

Combinational circuits settle after a few steps. A latch settles too, because its feedback loop reaches a stable state. A JK flip-flop with J = K = 1 and the clock high never settles: each step flips the output, which feeds back and flips it again. settle() reports that, and the page shows it as the race-around condition. Nothing about race-around is hard-coded; it comes out of the gate delays.

A clean clock pulse for Experiment 6

lab6.js · lines 23–27function settleLatch(c) {
    if (c.settle().stable) return;
    for (let k = 0; k < 4; k++) c.gates.forEach(g => { c.values[g.id] = OPS[g.type](g.in.map(sig => c.get(sig))) ? 1 : 0; });
    c.settle();
}

A short pulse holds the clock high for two gate delays: long enough for the steering gate and the latch to change once, too short for the new output to come back round. Tests (chapter 13) confirm every J, K, Q combination gives the right next state.

If both latch outputs end up equal, a unit-delay model would oscillate for ever. Real gates are never exactly equal in speed, so the tie is broken with a few in-order passes, the same way the simulator does when a circuit is first built.

The diode operating point

lab3.js · lines 17–30export function diodeReading(Vs, reverse) {
    if (reverse) {
        const I = (0.005 * (1 - Math.exp(-Vs)) + 0.0016 * Vs) * 1e-6; // nA–µA leakage
        return { Vd: Math.max(0, Vs - I * R), I };
    }
    let lo = 0;
    let hi = Math.max(0, Vs);
    for (let k = 0; k < 80; k++) {
        const mid = (lo + hi) / 2;
        if (mid + IS * (Math.exp(mid / (N * VT)) - 1) * R > Vs) hi = mid; else lo = mid;
    }
    const Vd = (lo + hi) / 2;
    return { Vd, I: (Vs - Vd) / R };
}

The current through the series resistor must equal the diode current from the Shockley equation. There is no closed-form answer, so the diode voltage is found by bisection: 80 halvings of the interval 0 to Vs, which is far more precise than the meter shows.

The ideality factor and saturation current (n ≈ 1.9, Is = 5 nA) put the knee of the simulated 1N4007 at about 0.6–0.7 V, like the real diode.

12 · Code walkthrough (continued)

Nodal analysis for the two-source network

dc.js · lines 13–19export function solveNetwork({ V1, V2, R1, R2, R3 }) {
    const VA = (V1 / R1 + V2 / R3) / (1 / R1 + 1 / R2 + 1 / R3);
    const I1 = (V1 - VA) / R1; // left source → node A
    const I3 = (V2 - VA) / R3; // right source → node A
    const I2 = VA / R2; // node A → ground
    return { VA, I1, I2, I3 };
}

Node A is the only unknown, so KCL at A gives one equation: the currents in from both sources equal the current down through R2. Solving it gives VA, and every branch current follows from Ohm's law.

The page then adds up the currents at the node and the voltages around each loop and prints the sums, which is how it demonstrates KCL and KVL.

Series AC circuits

ac.js · lines 65–74export function seriesAC({ type, R, L, C, V, f }) {
    const w = TAU * f;
    const XL = type.includes('L') ? w * L : 0;
    const XC = type.includes('C') ? 1 / (w * C) : 0;
    const X = XL - XC;
    const Z = Math.hypot(R, X);
    const theta = Math.atan2(X, R);
    const I = V / Z;
    return { XL, XC, X, Z, theta, I, VR: I * R, VL: I * XL, VC: I * XC, pf: Math.cos(theta), P: V * I * Math.cos(theta), Q: V * I * Math.sin(theta), S: V * I };
}

Reactances come from ω = 2πf. The impedance is the magnitude of R + jX, and the phase angle its argument. Power factor, real, reactive and apparent power follow. The same function drives the phasor diagram and the resonance sweep.

What I learned

  • Writing the model forces you to understand the topic. To make the diode bench give sensible readings I had to solve the diode and resistor together, which is exactly the load-line idea from Unit 1. To make the flip-flop toggle once per pulse I had to think about propagation delay, which is what race-around is really about.
  • Real readings are not perfect. Adding resistor tolerance and meter noise to Experiment 2 made the KVL sums come out at a few millivolts instead of zero, which is what happens on the real bench and is worth discussing in the viva.
  • Pin numbers matter. Drawing the 7402 made it obvious why circuits copied from a 7400 diagram fail: the NOR chip has its outputs on different pins.
  • The two syllabus versions overlap a lot. Mapping ECE249 and ECE131 side by side showed that the newer course trades digital electronics for circuits, machines and op-amps, so both were built into one site.
